Relationship to pain

This week is about getting familiar. No need to fix anything: just meeting the feelings around pain with curiosity and kindness.

Emotional inquiry

Joe Hudson · Art of Accomplishment

Practice outline

1
Settle
Feet — weight, gravity, soften. Pelvic floor settling. Diaphragm moving, relaxing. Bottom of the throat: let the back of the neck rest on that foundation.
2
Visit the neck and hands
If something is there, three questions: what shape, how big, what temperature. Find the exact line where the sensation ends and you begin. Notice that the pain alarm is real, but that its increased since the diagnosis -- but nothing has changed structurally. If there's no pain present, great.
3
Feel the feelings around the pain
Find the vigilant feeling — the one who has been on the lookout for discomfort since the diagnosis. Where does it live in your body? Step inside it and look around. Then one playful move: wave at it, or thank it for getting you to the doctor. Appreciate its care.
4
Rest
Find something pleasurable in the body. Rest there for a few breaths.
5
Welcome it, then play
From within it, welcome it completely. Then play with it: listen (what does it want to say?), tickle it, poke it, ignore it, push on it, resist it, wave at it, hug it, welcome it back, and thank it for its service. Finish with gratitude.
